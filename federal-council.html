        Governing council

        The Federal Council is the supreme deliberative body of the Middle Eastern Federation.

        Established by the compact of 1924, it comprises fifteen members: the Head of State, who presides; the governors of Turkey and Kurdistan; and six delegates from each member state. Governors are chosen within their states; delegates are returned by local assemblies. The Council is thus both a federal cabinet and a congress of the member states.

        The Head of State, Mustafa Iodinm, prepares the federal estimates, nominations and legislative program. No war may be declared, no federal tax levied, no new member admitted and no article amended without concurring votes. Removal of the Head of State requires ten votes of the fourteen others, so that neither delegation can impose its will alone. In tied divisions the chair has a casting vote except on his own removal.

        Procedure and powers

        In ordinary legislation a proposal is read, committed to the state delegations for report, and put to a roll of states. Governors countersign measures affecting police, courts, schools and religious endowments within their jurisdiction and are answerable for the delivery of their military quotas. Sessions are held in Istanbul; extraordinary sessions may be summoned in Ankara or Van.
